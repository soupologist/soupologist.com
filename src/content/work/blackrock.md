Worked on internal tooling for an attribution platform. Built an LLM-powered codebase exploration tool using tree-sitter and Google ADK.

## good memories
I have very fond memories of my time in Mumbai. 

I had very different expectations of how the day-to-day would be at BlackRock thinking that I'd have to show up in formals everyday, but it was surprisingly reasonably chill. 

### table tennis
I think my love for table tennis was properly nurtured here. At around 4PM everyday me and the other interns would look at each other and know that it was TT time. We used to play each other, and against other employees there: both interns and full-time folks. I did improve a lot as well over this time, and figure out my style of play.

## What I didn't quite like

### Remote Teams 
Looking back, something that really didn't work for me was the fact that my entire team and manager were in Gurgaon while I was in Mumbai. The human communication aspect of working is something I really enjoy and while I did talk a lot with my fellow interns as well as others in the Mumbai office, I didn't have quite the same interaction with the teammates specifically in my team over in Gurgaon.

Over time I did figure out that regular video calls and catchups were essential to stay in touch and build camaraderie with folks that far away, but it still didn't quite replicate the same connection I had with people of other teams who was physically around me.

### how hard it was to use new tools
Another thing to note was how difficult it was to do something different. So since I was building an AI agent with embeddings, naturally I'd need to have a vector database, and for local purposes I used ChromaDB. But eventually the time and need came for a hosted vectordb on the cloud so that other people can use and test out my tool as well, but there really was no option to use that made sense.

We weren't allowed to use even free versions of third party vectordbs and my mentors suggested using the vector part of MySQL in order to do it, but that's not really the same as a dedicated vectordb.
