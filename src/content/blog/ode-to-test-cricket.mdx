So India is back to playing competitive test cricket after a while, with the current tour of New Zealand, consisting of two matches. And in January, we have the home Border-Gavaskar Trophy which will go on till the beginning of March. So with a solid spell of tests coming up, I wanted to put my thoughts to pen and truly express why this is one of my favourite formats in sport.

## laid back
You'll realise very quickly that most of the time, nothing actually happens in the match, with most balls resulting in dots or singles, with the occasional drive for a boundary here and there. But there are certain periods sprinkled in throughout where suddenly it gets very interesting: the first few overs of an innings or the last over before the end of a session, for example. 

So the key thing is to note that you can't actively watch the entirety of a test match, considering that it's literally 6 hours a day upto 5 days. You just have in the background and go about your work, occasionally tuning in whenever something interesting happens. I usually even have the sound on because the ambience of the crowd is quite nice, and the commentary is usually very relaxed as well. When something worth your attention comes up, the crowd and commentators will let you know.

## bowling focused
With the absolute roads they're serving up in T20s nowadays, it's actually quite refreshing to see bowlers have the upper hand in test matches. The ball can either swing, seam, or turn wildly as the pitch ages. The wickets too are quite aesthetic to watch with most dismissals being caught behind or bowled. 

With that being said, even good batting is quite fun to watch as well with drives and flicks. And then all of a sudden in comes Rishabh Pant and flips the whole idea of test batting on its head with his aggressive counterattack and swashbuckling shots. 

## it's a unique format
I can't think of any sports that are played as long as test cricket is, with each match taking upto 5 days, with all four results possible (Win, Loss, Tie, Draw). And usually these matches are played as part of a series, so there is an underlying narrative behind each series. For example, India had never won a test series in Australia until [Virat Kohli's bunch made history in 2018-19](https://www.cricbuzz.com/cricket-series/2699/india-tour-of-australia-2018-19/matches), and Rahane's men proceeded to [do it again in 2020-21](https://www.cricbuzz.com/cricket-series/3213/india-tour-of-australia-2020-21/matches) in a thrilling series. 

I know that it sounds incredibly anticlimatic to witness 5 days of cricket only for it to end in a draw, but in some contexts it gets very interesting. There are often matches where you are in a losing position and simply batting out the clock can lead to a draw and salvage the team to fight for the series another day. That's exactly what happened in the [third test of the afforementioned BGT 2020-21](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scores/31647/aus-vs-ind-3rd-test-india-tour-of-australia-2020-21) when Ashwin and Vihari put together a historic partnership of 259 balls to force a draw. This allowed them the chance to win the fourth test and clinch the series.

There's lots of quirks with each country, like the subcontinent favouring spin, and the pitches in Australia providing a lot of bounce and seam, while the ones in England are known for being greentops and offering a lot of swing. Each ground has its own legacy and might have one or two historic matches played there. 

## the players clearly like it the most
I don't think there's much doubt in saying that the players always prioritise Test cricket, with Bumrah literally not playing the ODIs the year before the world cup, but he's right there when it comes to the test matches. With the World Test Championship system, every single test counts and it's actually quite competitive and mathematical to qualify for the final at Lord's.
